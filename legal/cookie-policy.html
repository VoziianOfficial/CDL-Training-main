02

What Cookies Are

A cookie is a small text record that a website may ask a browser to store on a device. When the browser later accesses the website, the stored value may be read to recognize a session, remember a preference, maintain security, or support another configured function.

Session cookies

Session cookies are generally designed to expire when the browser session ends. They may support temporary navigation, security, form processing, or other short-term functions.

Persistent cookies

Persistent cookies remain until their configured expiration, until they are replaced, or until the user removes them. They may remember preferences, consent choices, or other information between visits.

Cookie contents

A cookie commonly contains an identifier, status, preference, timestamp, or another limited value. It does not need to contain a person’s name to be associated with a browser or device.

Cookies cannot independently read arbitrary files stored on your device. Their behavior depends on the website, browser, script, and permissions involved.
03

Local Storage and Similar Tools

Websites may use technologies other than traditional cookies to store values, communicate with browser features, or measure interactions.

Local storage

Local storage allows a website to retain browser-based values without automatically sending them with every request. It may be used to remember consent preferences, interface choices, or other settings.

Session storage

Session storage is generally limited to the current browser tab or browsing session and may support temporary interface, navigation, or form-related behavior.

Pixels and tags

A pixel or tag may record that a page, email, image, or interface element was loaded or interacted with. These technologies may be used by analytics or advertising tools only when implemented and legally permitted.

Scripts and software development libraries

Website scripts may provide sliders, animations, accessibility, form validation, security, analytics, or preference controls. Loading a script may involve a request to a content delivery network or service provider.

Server logs

Hosting and security infrastructure may record technical request information without relying on a browser cookie. These records may include request time, page, browser, connection, security, and diagnostic information.

Essential Technologies

Essential technologies support functions reasonably necessary to provide, secure, and administer the website.

They may be used for purposes such as:

  • Remembering whether the consent interface was completed.
  • Storing the categories selected in the preference tool.
  • Supporting security and abuse prevention.
  • Protecting inquiry forms against automated submissions.
  • Maintaining temporary session or navigation state.
  • Supporting accessibility and interface operation.
  • Delivering requested website content.
  • Detecting technical errors or service failures.
  • Applying load balancing or infrastructure controls.

Blocking essential technologies may cause the website, consent interface, forms, or other necessary features to work incorrectly.

Consent Record A browser value may be stored to remember your preference selection and avoid displaying the consent prompt on every page visit.

Essential status does not permit a technology to be used for unrelated optional analytics or advertising purposes.

06

Preference Technologies

Preference technologies may remember optional choices that improve convenience but are not always necessary to provide basic informational content.

Examples may include:

  • Remembering a selected display or interface option.
  • Retaining a preferred content or program selection.
  • Remembering optional accessibility-related preferences.
  • Preserving a nonessential user-interface state.
  • Avoiding repeated display of a nonessential informational message.

If preference storage is disabled, the website may still operate, but the relevant choice may need to be selected again during a later visit.

Preference Scope Preference technologies should not be reclassified as essential merely because they make the website more convenient.

Analytics and Performance Tools

Analytics technologies may help understand how the website performs and how visitors generally interact with its pages and features.

Depending on the configured tool, analytics may process:

  • Pages viewed and navigation order.
  • Approximate visit duration.
  • Referring page or campaign information.
  • Browser, device, operating system, and screen category.
  • General geographic region inferred from connection data.
  • Interactions with buttons, sections, forms, or sliders.
  • Page speed, loading errors, and technical performance.
  • Aggregated traffic and content-use patterns.

Analytics information may help identify confusing navigation, broken functionality, slow pages, inaccessible behavior, or content that requires improvement.

Optional activation

Where consent is required, optional analytics should not load until the appropriate permission has been recorded.

Data minimization

Analytics configuration should limit information to what is reasonably necessary, use available privacy controls, and avoid collecting form-message contents or sensitive inquiry details for general measurement.

Measurement Notice This policy does not confirm that a particular analytics provider is currently active. The live website configuration should be reviewed before publication.

Advertising and Attribution Tools

If advertising technologies are implemented, they may support campaign attribution, frequency controls, advertising measurement, audience analysis, or personalization.

Depending on the tool and selected preferences, advertising technologies may process:

  • Browser or device identifiers.
  • Advertising interaction information.
  • Referral, campaign, or attribution parameters.
  • Pages viewed before or after an advertising interaction.
  • General device, browser, or geographic information.
  • Whether a specified website action occurred.

Advertising tools should not receive the full contents of a private inquiry message merely to measure a website conversion.

Personalized advertising

Personalized advertising may involve combining information across websites, services, browsers, or devices. It should be used only when legally permitted, properly disclosed, and consistent with the visitor’s valid choices.

Non-personalized measurement

Some advertising systems may offer restricted or non-personalized configurations. The legal status and available controls depend on the actual provider, jurisdiction, and implementation.

Implementation Requirement Advertising technologies must not be described as active unless they are actually implemented on the live website.

External links

Following an external link takes you to a service controlled by another organization. Its cookies and storage practices are governed by its own policy rather than this Cookie Policy.

Browser and Device Settings

Most modern browsers provide controls for viewing, deleting, limiting, or blocking cookies and website storage.

Depending on the browser, settings may allow you to:

  • Delete cookies and local storage for a specific website.
  • Clear all browser cookies and stored website data.
  • Block all cookies.
  • Block third-party cookies.
  • Permit cookies only for selected websites.
  • Delete stored information when the browser closes.
  • Use private or incognito browsing modes.
  • Control site permissions and tracking protection.

Consequences of blocking storage

Blocking all cookies or browser storage may prevent the website from remembering consent choices, maintaining temporary state, securing forms, or providing other necessary functions.

Multiple browsers and devices

Preferences are often stored separately for each browser, browser profile, device, and domain. A choice made on one device may not automatically apply elsewhere.

Clearing browser data

Clearing browser data may remove the stored consent record. The website may then display the consent interface again.

Browser Documentation Consult the help documentation for your browser or device for instructions that match the current software version.

Storage Duration

Browser technologies may remain for different periods depending on their function, configuration, provider, category, and legal requirements.

Session duration

Session technologies generally expire when the browser or relevant tab session ends, although browser behavior may vary.

Persistent duration

Persistent technologies remain until their configured expiration, until they are replaced, or until you remove them.

Consent duration

A consent record may remain long enough to remember the selected settings and policy version. A new choice may be requested after expiration or a material configuration change.

Provider duration

Third-party tools may use their own storage periods. Those periods should be reviewed and reflected accurately in any live cookie inventory.
