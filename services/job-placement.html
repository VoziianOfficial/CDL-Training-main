Employer Research

Know What Employers Request

A useful job search compares the role against your current license, endorsements, experience, location, availability, and preferred commercial driving direction.

License Requirements

Confirm whether the role requires Class A, Class B, specific restrictions, or additional endorsements.

Vehicle and Equipment

Review the commercial vehicles, transmission, trailer, passenger, cargo, or specialized equipment connected to the role.

Route and Schedule

Compare local, regional, longer-distance, daytime, overnight, weekend, rotating, or seasonal expectations.

Experience Standards

Identify whether the employer accepts entry-level applicants or requires specific commercial driving experience.

Driving History

Review the employer’s stated standards for licensing, violations, incidents, documentation, and insurability.

Application Process

Understand required documents, screening steps, assessment, orientation, background review, and follow-up expectations.

Career Direction Comparison

Route Options

Different driving roles may involve different equipment, schedules, route patterns, time away from home, loading responsibilities, and experience requirements.

Comparison — 01 / 02

Local Direction

Defined local area, more regular return pattern Discuss Local Roles

Local Direction at a Glance

  • Route Pattern Defined local area — routes may include delivery, transit, service, distribution, municipal, or terminal-based work
  • Schedule Potentially more regular return pattern — daily start and finish expectations still vary by employer, route, shift, and workload
  • Equipment Class A or Class B vehicles — straight trucks, buses, service vehicles, day cabs, trailers, or other local equipment
  • Work Responsibilities More stops or customer interaction — loading, unloading, route service, passenger, or delivery responsibilities

Comparison — 02 / 02

Regional Reach

Broader coverage, potential time away from home Discuss Regional Roles

Regional or Longer-Distance at a Glance

  • Route Pattern Broader geographic coverage — routes may cross regions or states and involve longer driving periods or multiple stops
  • Schedule Potential time away from home — trip length, overnight requirements, reset periods, and route schedules depend on the role
  • Equipment Commonly tractor-trailer focused — equipment may vary by freight, trailer type, route, employer, and endorsement requirements
  • Work Responsibilities Longer driving segments — freight movement, route planning, inspection, documentation, and extended road operation
